## 3.1 Business Model Canvas

### Customer Segments

| Segment | Description | Size / Importance |
|---------|-------------|------------------|

## 3.2 Business Model Strength Assessment

| Dimension | Rating | Rationale |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Revenue diversification | ★★★☆☆ | Two engines (iPhone + Services); Wearables maturing |
| Pricing power | ★★★★★ | ASP record $1,032; Pro mix accelerating; no price deflation |
| Customer lock-in | ★★★★★ | Ecosystem switching cost is among highest in consumer tech |
| Margin profile | ★★★★☆ | 46.9% blended gross, improving; Services diluting hardware |
| Capital efficiency | ★★★★★ | ROIC 52%+; FCF $98.8B; best-in-class |
| Platform network effects | ★★★★☆ | App Store has strong developer lock-in but DMA weakening EU |
| Geographic concentration | ★★★☆☆ | 15.5% China exposure; regulatory risk in EU |

## Impact Analysis

**So What**: Apple's business model is a premium-hardware-enabled platform — the iPhone is the acquisition channel, and Services is the monetization engine. This creates a powerful two-sided flywheel: more iPhones → more App Store/iCloud users → more developers → better apps → more iPhone purchases.

**Now What**: The critical design principle is "keep iPhone ASP rising to fund the flywheel." iPhone 17 Pro's $1,099 starting price, plus storage upgrades at 75% margin, shows the flywheel is functioning. Any hardware ASP compression (tariff pass-through, China competition) would stress the model.

**What If**: If developers increasingly bypass the App Store (post-DMA third-party stores; Epic injunction impact in the US), the commission revenue stream erodes. However, Apple's transaction take rate structure means volume growth can partially offset rate compression.

**Compared To**: Apple's BMC most closely resembles Razor-and-Blade (iPhone = razor, Services = blade) but with the unusual feature that the "razor" itself generates strong margins. This is superior to traditional razor/blade economics where the hardware is sold at a loss.
